Understanding the GRACE datasets

TWSA is terrestrial water storage anomaly: how much estimated total land-water storage differs from a baseline average, expressed as centimeters of equivalent water thickness. It is not the water depth of a flood.

Downscaled ensemble (0.05°), tws_m: the monthly ensemble mean of mass-conserving downscaled CSR, GSFC and JPL mascon estimates. Finer grid patterns are model-dependent, not independent satellite measurements at 5-km resolution. The downscaled file specifies a 2004–2009 reference baseline.

Thailand downscaled ensemble (nominal 1 km), tws_m_1km: Thailand-specific mass-conserved downscaled GRACE/GRACE-FO ensemble mean. Only tws_m is used from each 1-km NetCDF file. The finer grid is a model-derived estimate, not an independently measured 1-km GRACE resolution.

Original interpolated ensemble (0.05°), obs_m: average of the original three mascon estimates interpolated onto the downscaled grid for like-for-like maps. Interpolation is only a grid conversion: it does not create more independent GRACE measurements.

CSR mascon (0.25°, RL06.3): University of Texas Center for Space Research solution. Estimates gravity-driven terrestrial mass changes using mascon processing, with corrections such as geocenter, low-degree coefficients and glacial isostatic adjustment in the public corrected product. A 0.25° output grid is not equivalent to 0.25° independent resolution. CSR documentation.

GSFC mascon (0.5°, RL06v2.0): NASA Goddard Space Flight Center's mascon estimate. The distributed 0.5° equal-angle grid represents a coarser mass-estimation solution; spatial constraints help reduce noise and leakage. GSFC documentation.

JPL mascon (0.5°, RL06.3Mv04): NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory's mascon solution for changes in water, land ice and other mass stores, displayed here as equivalent water thickness anomalies. The product and any coastal-leakage/CRI options should be checked in the original file metadata. JPL documentation.

Versions: release labels above reflect the expected versions for the named source files. Confirm each file's attributes before citing. The three source grids are retained in this explorer, even when their images are rendered more sharply.

Map color limits: change the negative and positive limits in the layer panel. Default −50 to +50 cm; a wider range makes more values appear near the central pale color. Changing the palette does not change underlying TWSA or the chart.

Geographic mask: map colors are restricted to the downscaled GRACE land footprint (optionally intersected with your Southeast Asia polygon), including native CSR, GSFC and JPL layers. Other locations remain transparent.

Missing observations: months without valid satellite data remain blank. Hover over the time-series chart to read values and click a date to select that month; no temporal values are invented.
GRACE EXPLORER · QUICK GUIDE

Welcome to GRACE Explorer

See how water stored across South and Southeast Asia changes over time.

You can explore the map in just a few steps:

  1. 1
    Choose a water storage map

    Choose Original interpolated (5 km), Downscaled (5 km), or Thailand downscaled (1 km) from the large buttons on the left. The 1-km option appears only when those files were included in the build.

  2. 2
    Travel through time

    Choose a year and month at the bottom, or press ▶ Play to watch changes. Months without observations are left blank.

  3. 3
    Explore a country, province, or basin

    Hover to see its name. Click or double-click a boundary to open its easy-to-read Time series, with available datasets and trends selected. For spatial maps, use Technical use.

  4. 4
    Need more tools?

    Open Technical use for CSR, GSFC and JPL layers, detailed spatial maps, lightweight time-series charts, metrics, and downloads.

Reading the colors

Warm colors indicate below-average water storage; blue colors indicate above-average storage. These are anomalies relative to a baseline, not floodwater depths.
